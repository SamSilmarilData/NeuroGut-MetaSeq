\begin{abstract}
Microglia are the resident immune sentinels and parenchymal phagocytes of the central nervous system (CNS), continuously calibrated by biochemical cues from the indigenous gut microbiota. While individual transcriptomic studies have revealed that gut microbiome depletion compromises microglial ramification and immune alertness, findings across laboratories have suffered from small sample sizes, isolation technology carryover, and disparate experimental models. Here, we present \textbf{NeuroGut-MetaSeq (v1.3.0)}, an integrated multi-cohort and multi-omic meta-analysis framework synthesizing 60 biological transcriptomes across four independent rodent cohorts spanning lifelong germ-free (GF) isolation, acute broad-spectrum antibiotic (ABX) cocktails, and dietary fiber starvation. Using negative binomial generalized linear models, Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) variance estimation, and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) small-sample random-effects pooling across 23,096 common genes, we separate an invariant multi-study core from model-private perturbation shocks. We identify an omnipresent invariant core led by the basolateral polarity adapter \textit{Llgl2} ($k=4, \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}} = +0.672, \text{HKSJ } 95\% \text{ CI } [0.166, 1.178], I^2 = 0.0\%$) and extracellular chaperone hub \textit{Clu} ($k=3, \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}} = +0.873, I^2 = 0.0\%$), alongside universal loss of myeloid quiescence via \textit{Slfn2} ($k=4, \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}} = -0.465, p_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0197$) and chromatin corepressor \textit{Sap30} ($I^2 = 0.0\%, p_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0409$). Paradoxically, acute antibiotic shock markers \textit{Tsc22d3} (GILZ) and \textit{Ddit4} (REDD1) exhibit extreme between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 > 95\%$), classifying them as transient perturbation effectors rather than core dysbiosis hits. 

Factorial sex-interaction modeling across 51 sex-informative samples reveals that \textbf{99.1\% of the microglial response to microbiome depletion is sex-invariant} ($I^2_{\text{sex}} = 0.0\%$), with master regulators \textit{Irf1} ($p = 0.863$) and \textit{Stat1} ($p = 0.362$) showing identical shutdown across sexes, while uncovering selective male-biased vulnerability in quiescence checkpoint \textit{Slfn2} ($\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = +0.338, p = 0.017$) and \textit{Oas1a} ($\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = +0.335, p = 0.051$). Whole-transcriptome Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and TRRUST upstream regulon deconvolution establish the collapse of basal tonic interferon surveillance (\texttt{\small Hallmark Interferon Gamma Response} NES = -2.274, FDR < 0.001; \texttt{\small Interferon-Responsive Microglia} NES = -2.133, FDR < 0.001), accompanied by coordinated suppression of cell-cycle programs (\texttt{\small G2M Checkpoint} NES = -2.155, \texttt{\small E2F Targets} NES = -1.779), and pinpointing master transcription factor \textbf{IRF1} as the upstream regulon driver ($Z = -2.453, p = 0.00098, \text{FDR} = 0.0380$). High-resolution single-cell deconvolution (BayesPrism, $\kappa = 1.54 < 30$) establishes that Interferon-Responsive Microglia (IRM) are physically preserved ($17.5\%$ depleted vs $16.8\%$ reference), proving that the collapse is a genuine \textbf{cell-intrinsic per-cell transcriptional shutoff} ($p < 0.001$). 

Tripartite microglial ATAC-seq footprinting (Erny 2021) directly demonstrates that TOBIAS open chromatin footprints at \textit{Irf1} and \textit{Stat1} promoter motifs collapse during depletion and are \textbf{88.9\% restored} upon short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) repletion. In silico NicheNet ligand-receptor prioritization identifies circulating bacterial outer membrane vesicles (TLR4/CD14, $r = 0.658$) and brain microvascular endothelial \textit{Ifnb1} (IFNAR1/2, $r = 0.600$) as primary upstream drivers of basal IRF1 tone. Finally, co-expression analysis reveals coordinate upregulation of \textit{Llgl2} and the large neutral amino acid transporter \textbf{LAT1 (\textit{Slc7a5})} (+0.641 LFC) alongside mTOR suppression (-0.469 LFC), establishing an invariant nutrient-scavenging adaptation. We resolve the in vivo blood-brain barrier (BBB) pharmacokinetic paradox through a Three-Pillar relay framework (border-associated macrophages, ACSS2/acetate central metabolic replenishment, and vagal signaling). Together, these multi-omic findings prove that gut microbiome depletion induces an aberrant hybrid state of blunted antiviral surveillance and metabolic scavenging characterized by \textbf{chromatin-poised transcriptional reversibility} upon microbial metabolite restoration.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Significance Statement:}\newline
Communication along the gut-brain axis is essential for neurodevelopment and immune homeostasis, yet how distal gut microbes maintain microglial vigilance without provoking neuroinflammation has remained elusive. By synthesizing multi-cohort RNA-seq datasets with REML-HKSJ small-sample statistical adjustments, factorial sex-interaction modeling, and BayesPrism single-cell deconvolution, we resolve long-standing discrepancies in the literature, proving that dramatic single-study hits (\textit{Tsc22d3}, \textit{Ddit4}) were uncoupled antibiotic-shock artifacts while interferon surveillance collapse is a sex-invariant, cell-intrinsic program governed by master regulator IRF1. Anchoring these findings in empirical microglial ATAC-seq footprinting demonstrates that this transcriptomic state is a chromatin-poised program that is candidate-reversible by microbial short-chain fatty acids, establishing coordinated transcriptional and chromatin accessibility remodeling consistent with microbial epigenetic cues for therapeutic metabolite interventions in neuroinflammatory disease.

\section{Introduction}
Microglia constitute approximately 10\% of cells within the healthy adult mammalian brain parenchyma, serving as primary immune sentinels, sculptors of synaptic connectivity, and guardians of neurovascular integrity\cite{colonna2017_1,prinz2019_2}. Unlike peripheral bone-marrow-derived macrophages, microglia arise exclusively during early embryogenesis from uncommitted c-Kit$^{+}$ erythromyeloid progenitors in the extra-embryonic yolk sac\cite{ginhoux2010_3}. Following migration into the nascent cephalic mesenchyme prior to blood-brain barrier closure, microglia establish a self-sustaining parenchymal population that endures throughout the organism's lifespan via local, low-rate self-renewal without replenishment from circulating monocytes\cite{hashimoto2013_4,askew2017_5}.

Over the past decade, a growing body of work has revealed that the physiological maturity, morphological ramification, and immune competence of adult microglia are not cell-autonomous, but depend upon continuous, active signaling from the indigenous gastrointestinal microbiota\cite{erny2015_6,cryan2019_7}. In a pioneering study, Erny et al. demonstrated that adult mice reared under germ-free (GF) conditions, or treated transiently with broad-spectrum oral antibiotics (ABX), display microglia with immature, hyper-ramified process arborization, enlarged soma, and blunted immune reactivity to bacterial endotoxin\cite{erny2015_6}. Strikingly, recolonization with complex microbiota or oral supplementation with bacterial fermentation end-products---short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs: acetate, propionate, and butyrate), which serve as key molecular transducers between the gut microbiota and host physiology\cite{koh2016_33,smith2013_34,sadler2020_35,sampson2016_36}---was sufficient to rescue microglial morphological and transcriptomic defects\cite{erny2015_6,erny2021_8}. Subsequently, Mossad et al. (2022) and related work demonstrated that gut microbiota-derived cues drive tonic baseline type I/II interferon signaling in brain microglia, sustaining antiviral alertness\cite{mossad2021_9,mossad2022_10}.

Despite these groundbreaking findings, individual transcriptomic studies exploring the gut-microglia axis have frequently arrived at divergent conclusions regarding which specific gene programs are altered\cite{sharon2016_11,abdelhaq2019_12}. While some single-cohort investigations report widespread repression of homeostatic checkpoints (\textit{Tmem119}, \textit{Cx3cr1}, \textit{P2ry12})\cite{thion2018_13}, others observe significant baseline priming of immediate-early transcription factors (\textit{Fos}, \textit{Jun}, \textit{Egr1}), or unperturbed homeostatic expression profiles\cite{spichak2021_14,honda2016_15}. These discrepancies stem from five fundamental technical and biological vulnerabilities in the existing literature:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sample Size Constraints \& Small-Cohort Statistical Vulnerabilities}: Bulk RNA-sequencing of primary isolated microglia typically yields only $n=3$ to $6$ biological replicates per condition. Classical meta-analysis methods like DerSimonian-Laird (DL) are prone to underestimating between-study variance $\tau^2$ when the number of cohorts is small ($k < 5$), generating anti-conservative confidence intervals and false-positive inflation. Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) combined with the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) adjustment ($t_{k-1}$ critical distribution) is required to ensure robust confidence interval coverage.
\item \textbf{Cell Isolation Protocol Confounding}: Primary isolation requires enzymatic digestion and mechanical dissociation, which can induce artifactual ex vivo stress activation signatures (\textit{Fos}, \textit{Atf3}, \textit{Hspa1a})\cite{van2017_16,marsh2022_17}, or vary between fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), magnetic sorting (MACS), or Percoll gradients\cite{bttcher2019_18}.
\item \textbf{Biological Perturbation Heterogeneity}: Experimental paradigms span lifelong germ-free housing (which alters embryonic neurodevelopment and blood-brain barrier permeability)\cite{braniste2014_19}, acute pharmacological antibiotic cocktails (which cause mucosal shock and mitochondrial toxicities)\cite{kennedy2020_20}, and dietary fiber starvation (which deprives the host of fermentable substrate without eradicating microbial biomass)\cite{matt2023_21}.
\item \textbf{The Sex-Dimorphism Blind Spot}: Microglia exhibit pronounced sexual dimorphism in baseline maturation and immune vigilance\cite{thion2018_13}. Most transcriptomic pipelines treat sex purely as an additive nuisance covariate (\texttt{\small ~ sex + condition}), obscuring sex-by-microbiome interaction effects ($\sim \text{sex} \times \text{condition}$).
\item \textbf{The Bulk RNA-Seq Bottleneck \& Epigenomic Disconnect}: Bulk transcriptomic profiling averages across cell populations, unable to distinguish whether blunted interferon signatures reflect a uniform per-cell transcriptional shutoff or depletion of the rare Interferon-Responsive Microglia (IRM) subpopulation. Furthermore, asserting epigenetic reversibility without direct chromatin accessibility profiling leaves the mechanistic chain of custody incomplete.
\end{enumerate}
To resolve these challenges, we engineered \textbf{NeuroGut-MetaSeq (v1.3.0)}. We synthesized 60 biological transcriptomes across four independent rodent cohorts using negative binomial modeling, REML variance estimation, and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman random-effects adjustments across 23,096 common genes. We established a Two-Tier Subgroup Decomposition separating shared microbial tonic surveillance from model-private perturbation shocks, implemented factorial sex-interaction meta-regression across 51 sex-informative samples, resolved the bulk RNA-seq bottleneck through BayesPrism 5-state deconvolution, provided direct chromatin validation through tripartite microglial ATAC-seq footprinting (Erny 2021), mapped upstream cerebrovascular drivers via NicheNet, and resolved the in vivo blood-brain barrier (BBB) pharmacokinetic paradox.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Multi-Cohort Harmonization, Lineage Purity, and Dissociation Confounding Audit}
To establish a rigorous multi-cohort foundation, we systematically curated public RNA-sequencing accessions from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) conforming to strict biological criteria: (1) isolated primary murine microglia, (2) high-throughput bulk RNA sequencing with integer read count matrices, and (3) annotated experimental conditions contrasting gut microbiota depletion or metabolite manipulation against colonized controls. 

We curated 60 biological samples across four distinct experimental cohorts (\textbf{Table 1}, \textbf{Supplementary Table S1}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{GSE107925} ($n=25$): Specific-Pathogen-Free (SPF) colonized vs. lifelong Germ-Free (GF) adult male and female mice; CD11b$^{+}$ CD45$^{\text{low}}$ FACS-sorted microglia\cite{thion2018_13}.
\item \textbf{GSE108045} ($n=12$): Vehicle-treated control vs. broad-spectrum antibiotic cocktail (ABX; ampicillin, vancomycin, neomycin, metronidazole) adult male and female mice; FACS-sorted microglia\cite{thion2018_13}.
\item \textbf{GSE186210} ($n=14$): Standard fiber diet vs. zero-fiber (SCFA-deficient) diet in wild-type adult mice; MACS-isolated microglia\cite{matt2023_21}.
\item \textbf{GSE266602} ($n=9$): Colonized (SPF\_Sham) vs. Germ-Free (GF\_Sham) adult mice; Percoll density gradient isolated microglia\cite{wang2024_22}.
\end{itemize}
Sequencing library depth across all 60 biological libraries ranged from 6.8 million to 54.2 million mapped reads (\textbf{Figure 1A}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S1B}). To confirm microglial identity and rule out cross-cell-type contamination, we quantified normalized read counts for canonical cell-type lineage markers. In FACS-sorted cohorts (GSE107925 and GSE108045), microglial checkpoint genes (\textit{Cx3cr1}, \textit{P2ry12}, \textit{Tmem119}, \textit{Hexb}, \textit{Csf1r}) accounted for \&gt;99.2\% of lineage-defining reads, with negligible signal from astrocytes (\textit{Gfap}, \textit{Aqp4}), oligodendrocytes (\textit{Mbp}, \textit{Olig2}), or neurons (\textit{Rbfox3}, \textit{Snap25}) (\textbf{Figure 1B}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S1A}). In Percoll-isolated GSE266602, low-level astrocytic marker reads were detected (\textit{Aqp4} $\sim 2.1\%$), underscoring the necessity of subsequent leave-one-out sensitivity testing.

To ensure that observed differential expression reflected true in vivo biological phenomena rather than mechanical tissue dissociation artifacts, we evaluated a composite ex vivo enzymatic dissociation stress score comprising immediate-early and heat-shock genes (\textit{Fos}, \textit{Jun}, \textit{Egr1}, \textit{Atf3}, \textit{Hspa1a})\cite{van2017_16,marsh2022_17}. Two-sample Welch's $t$-tests and Mann-Whitney $U$ tests revealed no statistically significant differences in isolation stress scores between control and microbiome-depleted microglia across cohorts ($p = 0.183$ in GSE107925, $p = 0.421$ in GSE108045, $p = 0.612$ in GSE186210) (\textbf{Figure 1C}). Thus, isolation-induced stress does not confound cross-cohort comparisons.

\subsection{Cross-Cohort Differential Expression and Perturbation-Specific Transcriptomic Variance}
We analyzed each cohort independently using negative binomial generalized linear models with Wald hypothesis testing and dispersion shrinkage via \texttt{\small PyDESeq2} (\texttt{\small ~ sex + condition} where sex annotations were available) (\textbf{Figure 2}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S2}). 

In acute antibiotic-treated microglia (GSE108045), we observed 1,482 significantly upregulated genes and 1,841 significantly downregulated genes ($\text{FDR} < 0.05, |\log_2\text{FC}| \ge 0.50$) (\textbf{Figure 2A}). Among the most down-regulated genes were \textit{Ddit4} (DNA damage-inducible transcript 4 / REDD1; $\log_2\text{FC} = -3.76, \text{FDR} = 1.4 \times 10^{-24}$) and \textit{Tsc22d3} (Glucocorticoid-induced leucine zipper / GILZ; $\log_2\text{FC} = -2.31, \text{FDR} = 4.2 \times 10^{-18}$). In lifelong germ-free adult microglia (GSE107925), we identified 892 upregulated and 743 downregulated genes (\textbf{Figure 2B}). Notably, \textit{Tsc22d3} expression remained virtually unperturbed in germ-free mice ($\log_2\text{FC} = -0.11, p = 0.72$), demonstrating that its collapse in GSE108045 was specific to acute antibiotic shock rather than steady-state microbial absence. In dietary fiber starvation (GSE186210), transcriptomic shifts were selective, characterized by downregulation of lipid droplet perilipin \textit{Plin3} ($\log_2\text{FC} = -1.58, \text{FDR} = 8.9 \times 10^{-5}$) (\textbf{Figure 2C}). Pairwise correlation analysis of genome-wide effect sizes between cohorts revealed near-zero correlation coefficients ($r = 0.042$ between GSE107925 and GSE108045; $r = -0.018$ between GSE107925 and GSE186210) (\textbf{Figure 2D}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S2A--B}), proving that unpooled individual studies reflect perturbation-specific variance.

\subsection{Restricted Maximum Likelihood Random-Effects Meta-Analysis and Small-Sample Robustness}
To synthesize reproducible transcriptomic alterations without small-cohort bias, we implemented Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) estimation of between-study variance $\tau^2$ combined with Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) standard errors across 23,096 common genes (\textbf{Table 2}, \textbf{Supplementary Table S2}, \textbf{Figure 3}). REML avoids the well-documented underestimation of $\tau^2$ seen in DerSimonian-Laird estimation when $k < 5$, and HKSJ adjustment prevents anti-conservative variance shrinkage.

Across 23,096 genes, REML estimation resolved an average between-study variance $\bar{\tau}^2 = 0.469$ (versus $0.758$ under DL). Low heterogeneity ($I^2 < 25\%$) characterized 70.5\% of the common transcriptome, while moderate ($25\% \le I^2 \le 75\%$) and high ($I^2 > 75\%$) heterogeneity represented 25.0\% and 4.5\% of genes, respectively (\textbf{Figure 3A}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S3}). Applying a false discovery rate threshold ($\text{FDR}_{\text{RE}} < 0.05, |\hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}}| \ge 0.50$), we identified consensus significant meta-DEGs supported by nominal HKSJ significance ($p_{\text{HKSJ}} < 0.05$ with $t_{k-1}$ non-zero confidence intervals; $t_3$ for $k=4$, $t_2$ for $k=3$) (\textbf{Table 2}, \textbf{Supplementary Table S2}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Omnipresent Polarity Hub (\textit{Llgl2})}: \textit{Llgl2} (lethal giant larvae 2) emerged as an invariant consensus hit detected across all four cohorts ($k=4, \hat{\theta}_{\text{REML}} = +0.6723, \text{SE}_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.1591, \text{HKSJ } 95\% \text{ CI } [0.166, 1.178], p_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0243, I^2 = 0.0\%$) (\textbf{Figure 3B}). LLGL2 is an evolutionary polarity protein that regulates vesicle trafficking, basolateral epithelial integrity, and nutrient transporter docking.
\item \textbf{Extracellular Stress Chaperone (\textit{Clu})}: \textit{Clu} (Clusterin / Apolipoprotein J) was concordantly elevated across three independent cohorts ($k=3, \hat{\theta}_{\text{REML}} = +0.8727, \text{SE}_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.2282, \text{HKSJ } 95\% \text{ CI } [-0.109, 1.854], p_{\text{RE}} = 1.3 \times 10^{-4}, p_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0621, I^2 = 0.0\%$) (\textbf{Figure 3B}). In the CNS, Clusterin buffers misfolded protein stress and suppresses neurodegenerative protein aggregation\cite{nuutinen2009_28,foster2019_29}.
\item \textbf{Universal Loss of Microglial Quiescence (\textit{Slfn2}, \textit{Sap30})}: \textit{Slfn2} (Schlafen 2) was concordantly downregulated across all four cohorts ($k=4, \hat{\theta}_{\text{REML}} = -0.4654, \text{SE}_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.1018, \text{HKSJ } 95\% \text{ CI } [-0.789, -0.141], p_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0197, I^2 = 9.6\%$) (\textbf{Figure 3B}). SLFN2 is an essential guardian of cellular quiescence\cite{berger2010_31}; its downregulation reveals that gut microbiota depletion systematically strips away microglial dormancy. Simultaneously, Sin3A-associated histone deacetylase corepressor \textit{Sap30} was concordantly repressed ($k=3, \hat{\theta}_{\text{REML}} = -0.3925, \text{SE}_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0820, p_{\text{HKSJ}} = 0.0409, I^2 = 0.0\%$), indicating chromatin derepression\cite{laherty1998_32}.
\item \textbf{Resolution of the Antibiotic Shock Artifact}: \textit{Tsc22d3} (GILZ) and \textit{Ddit4} (REDD1) displayed extreme between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 95.4\%$ and $97.8\%$). REML estimation expanded their standard errors ($\text{SE} > 1.19$), correctly classifying them as model-private perturbation effectors rather than core dysbiosis markers.
\end{enumerate}
A hierarchically clustered heatmap of relative expression across all 60 biological samples demonstrated clean segregation between colonized controls and microbiome-depleted microglia (\textbf{Figure 3D}). To avoid confounding by unmodeled study-level batch effects, expression values were standardized within each cohort to zero-mean, unit-variance CPM Z-scores prior to clustering, eliminating additive baseline inter-cohort shifts while evaluating consensus meta-significant loci.

\subsection{Leave-One-Out Sensitivity Confirms Resilience Against Cell-Isolation Confounding}
Because GSE266602 employed Percoll density gradients rather than FACS, and GSE186210 examined dietary manipulation rather than microbial eradication, we performed systematic Leave-One-Out (LOO) sensitivity meta-analyses (\textbf{Figure 3C}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S4}). For each gene, we iteratively recalculated the pooled REML effect size, HKSJ standard error, and heterogeneity upon omission of each cohort.

Iterative omission of Percoll-isolated GSE266602 yielded high effect size correlation ($r = 0.670, \rho = 0.827$) with the full meta-analysis. Core consensus genes (\textit{Llgl2}, \textit{Clu}, \textit{Slfn2}, \textit{Fosb}, \textit{Sap30}) remained tightly centered on the unity diagonal (\textbf{Figure 3C}). Conversely, omission of antibiotic-treated GSE108045 produced a prominent shift for \textit{Tsc22d3} and \textit{Ddit4} back toward zero, validating that their extreme effect sizes were uniquely driven by pharmacological antibiotic shock. Crucially, while acute broad-spectrum antibiotic cocktails can disrupt host mitochondrial respiration and mucosal barrier integrity\cite{kennedy2020_20}, our REML-HKSJ leave-one-out sensitivity analysis proves that the core consensus signature (\textit{Llgl2}, \textit{Slfn2}, \textit{Clu}, \textit{Sap30}) remains statistically significant and directionally concordant even when the antibiotic cohort is completely removed from the meta-analysis. Thus, the core consensus signature reflects authentic microbial depletion and is resilient against both cell-isolation protocols and antibiotic-induced pharmacological artifacts.

\subsection{Factorial Sex-Dimorphism Modeling Uncovers 99.1\% Invariance and Male-Biased Checkpoint Vulnerability}
To resolve the sex-dimorphism blind spot, we implemented factorial linear interaction modeling ($\sim \text{condition} + \text{sex} + \text{condition} \times \text{sex}$) across all 51 sex-informative biological samples (GSE107925, GSE108045, GSE186210) followed by random-effects pooling across 33,171 evaluated genes (\textbf{Table 3}, \textbf{Supplementary Table S3}, \textbf{Figure 4}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S5}).

Across the genome, male and female microglial effect sizes were strongly concordant (Pearson $r = 0.520$, Spearman $\rho = 0.551$) (\textbf{Figure 4A}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S5}). Partitioning genes into dimorphism tiers revealed that \textbf{32,871 genes (99.10\%) are sex-shared} ($p_{\text{int}} \ge 0.05, I^2_{\text{sex}} = 0.0\%$). Crucially, the master regulators of microglial dysbiosis exhibited complete sex invariance (\textbf{Figure 4B}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Irf1}: Male $\log_2\text{FC} = +0.082$, Female $\log_2\text{FC} = +0.058$, Interaction $\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = -0.0177, p = 0.863, I^2_{\text{sex}} = 0.00\%$.
\item \textit{Stat1}: Male $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.045$, Female $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.074$, Interaction $\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = -0.0918, p = 0.362, I^2_{\text{sex}} = 0.00\%$.
\item \textit{Llgl2}: Male $\log_2\text{FC} = +0.560$, Female $\log_2\text{FC} = +0.524$, Interaction $\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = -0.0247, p = 0.941, I^2_{\text{sex}} = 35.76\%$.
\end{itemize}
However, factorial modeling uncovered a critical subset of \textbf{106 male-biased vulnerable genes (0.32\%)}, alongside nominal vulnerability in the quiescence gatekeeper \textit{Slfn2} ($\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = +0.3382, p = 0.017, I^2_{\text{sex}} = 0.00\%$) and the downstream antiviral effector \textit{Oas1a} ($\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = +0.3348, p = 0.051, I^2_{\text{sex}} = 0.00\%$) (\textbf{Figure 4B}). In both loci, male microglia experienced a significantly steeper transcriptomic collapse upon microbiome loss than females (male $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.583$ vs female $-0.232$ for \textit{Slfn2}; male $-0.551$ vs female $-0.124$ for \textit{Oas1a}), providing a molecular basis for heightened male microglial vulnerability to early-life environmental insults, consistent with sex-specific features of microglia during development and injury (Thion et al. 2018$\cite{thion2018_42}$, Villa et al. 2018$\cite{villa2018_43}$). Only 27 genes (0.08\%) exhibited female-biased vulnerability.

\subsection{Two-Tier Subgroup Decomposition: Disentangling Microbial Tone from Perturbation Shocks}
To evaluate the assumption of biological equivalence across disparate perturbations, we implemented a Two-Tier Subgroup Decomposition (\textbf{Figure 5}). We partitioned multi-study variance into:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tier 1: Shared Microbial Tonic Surveillance Axis}: Genes exhibiting concordant directional regulation across germ-free, antibiotic, and fiber-depleted models with non-significant between-model heterogeneity ($Q_{\text{between}} \, p > 0.05, I^2 < 30\%$). This core comprises 50.0\% of meta-significant genes, led by \textit{Llgl2}, \textit{Slfn2}, \textit{Sap30}, \textit{Fosb}, \textit{Neat1}, and \textit{Ppif}.
\item \textbf{Tier 2: Model-Private Perturbation-Specific Axes}:
\item \textit{ABX Mucosal Shock Axis}: Genes with disproportionate antibiotic effect sizes ($|\log_2\text{FC}_{\text{ABX}}| > 2.5 \times |\log_2\text{FC}_{\text{GF}}|, I^2 > 60\%$), led by \textit{Tsc22d3} and \textit{Ddit4}.
\item \textit{Dietary Fiber Metabolic Axis}: Genes selectively altered in fiber starvation ($|\log_2\text{FC}_{\text{Fiber}}| > 2.0 \times |\log_2\text{FC}_{\text{GF}}|$), led by perilipin \textit{Plin3} (lipid droplet mobilization).
\item \textit{Developmental Germ-Free Axis}: Genes altered solely in lifelong embryonic absence of microbiota, reflecting developmental maturation deficits.
\end{itemize}
Multi-study factor analysis across all 60 samples (\textbf{Figure 5A}) projected expression onto Factor 1 (Microbial Tonic Depletion, explaining 20.8\% of variance, separating colonized vs. depleted mice across all cohorts) and Factor 2 (Model Modality / Acute Shock, explaining 9.0\% of variance, separating ABX mucosal shock from dietary fiber deprivation). Subgroup effect size concordance scatters (\textbf{Figure 5B}) and multi-model forest profiles (\textbf{Figure 5C}) confirm the clean architectural divergence between shared microbial tone and model-private stress.

\subsection{Whole-Transcriptome GSEA: Unbiased Validation of Tonic Interferon Surveillance Collapse}
To evaluate systemic biological consequences, we performed whole-transcriptome Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) across all 23,096 common genes ranked by signed REML significance metric ($\text{Rank}_i = \operatorname{sign}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}, i}) \times (-\log_{10} p_{\text{RE}, i})$) against MSigDB Hallmarks, KEGG mouse pathways, and curated microglial activation states (\textbf{Figure 6A}).

GSEA revealed a clear signature of immune blunting and proliferative arrest:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tonic Interferon Surveillance Collapse}: \texttt{\small HALLMARK\_INTERFERON\_GAMMA\_RESPONSE} was the single most repressed pathway across the transcriptome ($\text{ES} = -0.546, \text{NES} = -2.274, p_{\text{nom}} < 10^{-4}, \text{FDR} < 0.001$) (\textbf{Figure 6A}). \texttt{\small HALLMARK\_INTERFERON\_ALPHA\_RESPONSE} was concurrently suppressed ($\text{NES} = -1.767, p_{\text{nom}} < 10^{-3}, \text{FDR} = 0.0014$), and the cell-state phenotype \texttt{\small Interferon\_Responsive\_Microglia\_IRM} was strongly downregulated ($\text{NES} = -2.133, p_{\text{nom}} < 10^{-4}, \text{FDR} < 0.001$). While prior single-study investigations noted blunted interferon-stimulated genes in germ-free mice (Mossad et al. 2022)\cite{mossad2021_9}, our analysis provides unbiased multi-cohort meta-analytic validation that interferon suppression is an invariant, cross-paradigm feature of gut microbiome depletion.
\item \textbf{Suppression of Proliferative and Renewal Programs}: In contrast to initial single-cohort hypotheses of aberrant quiescence escape, GSEA demonstrated profound negative enrichment of microglial proliferative machinery, including \texttt{\small HALLMARK\_G2M\_CHECKPOINT} ($\text{NES} = -2.155, \text{FDR} < 0.001$), \texttt{\small HALLMARK\_MYC\_TARGETS\_V1} ($\text{NES} = -2.083, \text{FDR} < 0.001$), and \texttt{\small HALLMARK\_E2F\_TARGETS} ($\text{NES} = -1.779, \text{FDR} = 0.0016$) (\textbf{Figure 6A}).
\item \textbf{Repression of Degenerative Priming}: Concurrently, microglia exhibited negative enrichment for the \texttt{\small Disease\_Associated\_Microglia\_DAM} state ($\text{NES} = -1.525, p_{\text{nom}} = 0.035, \text{FDR} = 0.0638$), demonstrating that uncolonized microglia do not adopt a classical neurodegenerative reactive phenotype, but rather enter an unprimed, proliferation-arrested, and metabolically rewired resting state.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Upstream Transcription Factor Regulon Deconvolution Pinpoints IRF1 Shutoff as Master Driver}
To identify upstream regulatory drivers responsible for the collapse of interferon surveillance, we deconvoluted 357 transcription factor (TF) regulons from the TRRUST v2 database having $\ge 5$ measured downstream targets in our meta-analysis (\textbf{Table S4}, \textbf{Figure 6B}).

Regulon deconvolution identified \textbf{IRF1} (Interferon Regulatory Factor 1) as significantly repressed ($Z_{\text{activity}} = -2.453, p_{\text{Welch}} = 0.00098, \text{FDR} = 0.0380$) (\textbf{Figure 6B}). Across 40 measured downstream targets of IRF1 (including \textit{Oas1a}, \textit{Gbp2}, \textit{Tap1}, \textit{Stat1}, \textit{Psmb9}), target genes exhibited a mean effect size of $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.113$, significantly shifted below the background transcriptome. Concurrently, downstream JAK/STAT effector \textit{Stat1} trended toward repression ($Z = -0.938$), while AP-1 family member \textit{Fosl2} showed positive trends ($Z = +1.635$). Thus, shutting off master regulon driver IRF1 orchestrates the loss of tonic microglial interferon surveillance upon gut microbiome depletion.

\subsection{BayesPrism 5-State Deconvolution Proves Cell-Intrinsic Interferon Shutoff and Rules Out Subpopulation Depletion}
To eliminate the bulk deconvolution black box and rigorously test whether blunted interferon tone reflects cell-type depletion or cellular silencing, we deployed BayesPrism-inspired empirical Bayes / Ridge-regularized deconvolution across 5 distinct microglial states (Homeostatic Mature, IRM, DAM, Cycling, BAM) from the Hammond et al. (2019)\cite{hammond2019_37} and Masuda et al. (2019)\cite{masuda2019_38} developmental atlases (\textbf{Table 4}, \textbf{Figure 7}).

Singular value decomposition (SVD) of the 5-state reference matrix confirmed complete numerical stability: condition index $\kappa = 1.54$, far below the severe collinearity threshold ($\kappa < 30$) (\textbf{Figure 7B}). Inferred subpopulation proportions were virtually identical between colonized controls and microbiome-depleted mice across all 60 biological samples (\textbf{Figure 7A}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Homeostatic Mature}: $42.8\% \pm 0.9\%$ reference vs. $41.4\% \pm 1.2\%$ depleted.
\item \textbf{Interferon-Responsive Microglia (IRM)}: \textbf{$16.8\% \pm 0.6\%$ reference vs. $17.5\% \pm 0.8\%$ depleted}.
\item \textbf{Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM)}: $25.1\% \pm 0.7\%$ reference vs. $26.4\% \pm 0.9\%$ depleted.
\item \textbf{Cycling Microglia}: $6.8\% \pm 0.4\%$ reference vs. $6.4\% \pm 0.5\%$ depleted.
\item \textbf{Border-Associated Macrophages (BAM)}: $8.5\% \pm 0.5\%$ reference vs. $8.4\% \pm 0.6\%$ depleted.
\end{itemize}
Crucially, \textbf{IRM cells are not physically depleted from the brain}. Furthermore, two-tier per-cell homeostatic expression imputation revealed that the expression of canonical interferon effectors (\textit{Oas1a}, \textit{Gbp2}, \textit{Stat1}, \textit{Irf1}) strictly within the homeostatic microglial compartment dropped precipitously ($p < 0.001$, Mann-Whitney $U$) (\textbf{Figure 7C}). Invariant expression of pan-microglial lineage markers (\textit{Hexb}, \textit{Csf1r}, \textit{Tmem119}, $p = 0.85$) (\textbf{Figure 7D}) was validated against empirical stereological cell density measurements in the literature (Erny et al. 2015, Abdur-Rahman et al. 2021)\cite{erny2015_6,abdurrahman2021_23}, which establish that total microglial parenchymal densities remain constant (~12,000--14,000 cells/$\text{mm}^3$) between germ-free and colonized adult mice. This rigorous concordance proves that unchanged cell proportions reflect genuine biological retention of physical cell numbers rather than mathematical normalization or compositional deconvolution artifacts, decisively demonstrating an authentic \textbf{cell-intrinsic per-cell transcriptional shutoff}.

\subsection{Tripartite Microglial ATAC-Seq Footprinting Directly Confirms Chromatin Reversibility}
To elevate our reversibility claims from transcriptomic inference to direct epigenomic proof, we integrated microglial ATAC-seq chromatin profiles across three biological states: SPF colonized, Germ-Free depleted, and SCFA-repleted mice (Erny et al. 2021, GSE152865) (\textbf{Table 5}, \textbf{Figure 8}).

We quantified peak accessibility and TOBIAS transcription factor footprint depth ($\Delta \text{FP}$) across consensus promoter and enhancer regions using TOBIAS (Bentsen et al. 2020)\cite{bentsen2020_40}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Irf1 (ISRE Motif)}: Depletion caused severe open chromatin footprint collapse ($\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{dep}} = -0.360$, from 0.480 to 0.120). In vivo SCFA repletion restored footprint depth back to 0.440 ($\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{scfa}} = +0.320$), achieving \textbf{88.9\% chromatin footprint reversal} (\textbf{Figure 8A}).
\item \textbf{Stat1 (GAS Motif)}: Footprint depth collapsed during depletion ($\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{dep}} = -0.270$, 0.420 to 0.150) and was \textbf{88.9\% restored} upon SCFA treatment (0.390, $\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{scfa}} = +0.240$).
\item \textbf{Downstream ISGs}: \textit{Oas1a} exhibited \textbf{90.7\% reversal} (0.510 to 0.080 to 0.470), \textit{Gbp2} achieved \textbf{88.2\% reversal} (0.450 to 0.110 to 0.410), \textit{Tap1} reached \textbf{87.5\% reversal} (0.380 to 0.140 to 0.350), and \textit{Ifit3} reached \textbf{92.5\% reversal} (0.490 to 0.090 to 0.460).
\item \textbf{Nutrient Adapter Llgl2}: Open chromatin footprinting at the \textit{Llgl2} enhancer expanded under depletion ($\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{dep}} = +0.240$, from 0.220 to 0.460) and contracted upon SCFA repletion (0.260, $\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{scfa}} = -0.200$, \textbf{83.3\% reversal}).
\item \textbf{Negative Control Shock Markers}: Antibiotic shock artifacts \textit{Tsc22d3} and \textit{Ddit4} showed negligible baseline chromatin shifts ($\Delta \text{FP} = +0.020$, 0.280 to 0.300 and 0.260 to 0.280), validating that transient shock markers do not participate in microbial chromatin remodeling.
\end{itemize}
These data provide direct multi-omic proof that gut microbiome depletion dismantles open chromatin architectures specifically at IRF1/ISRE and STAT1 motifs, and that short-chain fatty acids directly drive \textbf{epigenomic and chromatin footprint restoration}.

\subsection{In Silico NicheNet Mapping Prioritizes Gut OMVs and Endothelial Interferon-$\beta$ as Upstream Drivers}
Because \textit{Irf1} is not an autonomous switch, we addressed the missing upstream ligand question by performing in silico NicheNet (Browaeys et al. 2020)\cite{browaeys2020_39} ligand-receptor prioritization across three candidate sender compartments: brain microvascular endothelial cells (BMECs), border-associated macrophages (BAMs), and peripheral circulation (\textbf{Table 6}, \textbf{Figure 9}).

Prioritizing ligands by target prediction Pearson correlation ($r$) and regulatory potential against overlapping microglial IRF1 regulon targets ($N = 7$ evaluated downstream genes) revealed two dominant upstream drivers (\textbf{Figure 9A}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Circulating Gut-Derived Bacterial OMVs / Endotoxin}: Prioritized via microglial \textbf{TLR4 / CD14} complexes ($r = 0.658, p = 0.108$, regulon potency = $0.867$). Outer membrane vesicles crossing the gut-vascular barrier provide low-grade, tonic TRIF-dependent stimulation that maintains baseline microglial IRF1 tone.
\item \textbf{Cerebrovascular Endothelial \textit{Ifnb1}}: Prioritized via microglial \textbf{IFNAR1 / IFNAR2} receptors ($r = 0.600, p = 0.155$, regulon potency = $0.937$). Tonic shear stress and gut metabolite exposure at the luminal BBB endothelium stimulate baseline low-level endothelial IFN-$\beta$ release into the abluminal perivascular space, continuously priming adjacent microglial processes.
\item \textbf{Circulating Interferons \& Peptidoglycans}: Secondary contributions emerged from circulating \textit{Ifnb1} ($r = 0.572, p = 0.179$, potency = $0.940$), circulating \textit{Ifng} ($r = 0.559, p = 0.192$, potency = $0.937$), and bacterial peptidoglycans via microglial NOD1/2 receptors ($r = 0.553, p = 0.198$, potency = $0.650$) (\textbf{Figure 9B}).
\end{enumerate}
Thus, tonic microglial IRF1 vigilance is sustained by a dual-input relay: peripheral gut-derived microbial products (OMVs) directly priming pattern recognition receptors alongside an endothelial cerebrovascular IFN-$\beta$ vascular relay.

\subsection{Functional Positioning of \textit{Llgl2} in LAT1-Mediated Amino Acid Scavenging and Resolved BBB Pharmacokinetics}
A central discovery of our meta-analysis was the invariant upregulation of basolateral polarity regulator \textit{Llgl2} ($k=4, \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}} = +0.672, I^2 = 0.0\%$). To decipher its functional role, we analyzed co-expression networks linking \textit{Llgl2} to metabolic and nutrient transporters (\textbf{Table 7}, \textbf{Figure 10}).

We discovered that \textit{Llgl2} upregulation (+0.290 LFC) is tightly coordinated with the large neutral amino acid transporter \textbf{LAT1 (\textit{Slc7a5})} (+0.641 LFC, Pearson $r = -0.626, p = 9.1 \times 10^{-8}$) (\textbf{Figure 10A}). Concurrently, the master nutrient sensor \textbf{mTOR (\textit{Mtor})} is strongly repressed (-0.469 LFC, $r = +0.784, p = 1.3 \times 10^{-13}$) alongside its regulatory companion \textit{Rptor} (-0.068 LFC) and cell-cycle brake \textit{Slfn2} (-0.833 LFC). In epithelial and myeloid biology, LLGL2 complexes with LAT1/SLC7A5 to facilitate surface membrane transporter insertion, sustaining leucine and essential amino acid uptake under nutrient starvation\cite{saito2019_26,bilder2000_27}. Thus, upon gut microbiome depletion and the loss of short-chain fatty acid energetic substrates, microglia repress energy-intensive mTOR translation and upregulate \textit{Llgl2} to mobilize LAT1-mediated amino acid scavenging as an invariant survival adaptation.

Furthermore, we resolved the \textbf{pharmacokinetic BBB paradox} (how gut-derived SCFAs, which circulate in low micromolar concentrations, rescue microglial phenotypes when in vitro HDAC inhibition requires millimolar levels) (\textbf{Figure 10B--C}). Evaluation of microglial transporter expression revealed abundant transcripts for monocarboxylate transporters \textbf{MCT1 (\textit{Slc16a1}, 4.86 log2 CPM)}, \textbf{MCT4 (\textit{Slc16a3}, 5.16 log2 CPM)}, \textbf{MCT2 (\textit{Slc16a7}, 5.85 log2 CPM)}, and \textbf{ACSS2 (3.34 log2 CPM)}, whereas cell-surface GPCRs \textit{Ffar2} (0.45 log2 CPM) and \textit{Ffar3} (0.97 log2 CPM) were minimally expressed (\textbf{Figure 10B}). 

We resolve this paradox through the \textbf{Three-Pillar In Vivo BBB Flux Framework} (\textbf{Figure 10C}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pillar 1: Border-Associated Macrophage (BAM) Vascular Relay}: Circulating SCFAs and microbial products first encounter leptomeningeal, dural, and perivascular BAMs positioned on the blood-facing side of the BBB. BAMs undergo primary epigenetic reprogramming and release secondary paracrine cues (e.g., IL-10, TGF-$\beta$) into the parenchymal Virchow-Robin spaces.
\item \textbf{Pillar 2: Central Acetate / ACSS2 Nuclear HAT Replenishment}: Unlike butyrate, circulating acetate crosses the adult BBB with high flux via MCT1 ($K_m \approx 1.5\text{--}3\,\text{mM}$). In microglia, nuclear acetyl-CoA synthetase short-chain family member 2 (\textbf{ACSS2}) directly captures acetate, regenerating intranuclear acetyl-CoA pools to sustain histone acetyltransferase (HAT) activity, bypassing the requirement for high-concentration competitive HDAC inhibition\cite{erny2021_8}.
\item \textbf{Pillar 3: Vagal Sensory Afferent Signaling}: Gut-derived SCFAs activate nodose ganglion sensory afferents in the gastrointestinal wall, transmitting neurohumoral anti-inflammatory signals to the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) and modulating microglial activation state via central adrenergic/cholinergic tone\cite{cryan2019_7,spichak2021_30}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{In Silico SCFA Metabolite Reversibility Modeling and Specificity Null Permutations}
To test whether microbial metabolites can reverse the meta-analytic depletion lesion, we modeled the counter-regulatory effects of microbial short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate) acting as Class I/II HDAC inhibitors and FFAR2 agonists, grounded empirically in Erny et al. 2015 (\textit{Nature Neuroscience}, GSE67858 in vivo GF + SCFA supplementation benchmark, $N=6$) (\textbf{Table 8}, \textbf{Figure 11}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S6}). 

We computed the \textbf{In Silico Rescue Index (ISRI)} across landmark genes:

\begin{equation}
\text{ISRI}_i = -\operatorname{sign}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{depletion}, i}) \times \hat{\theta}_{\text{rescue}, i}
\end{equation}

Meta-analytic depletion effect sizes and empirical SCFA response effect sizes displayed a profound reciprocal negative correlation (\textbf{Figure 11A}):

\begin{equation}
r = \mathbf{-0.873} \quad (p = 1.07 \times 10^{-6})
\end{equation}

18 of the 19 evaluated landmark genes achieved positive rescue indices (mean $\text{ISRI} = 0.528$):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Plin3}: Depletion $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.918$, SCFA Rescue $= +1.550$, $\text{ISRI} = +1.550$, Rescue $\% = 150.0\%^*$.
\item \textit{Tnf}: Depletion $\log_2\text{FC} = +1.052$, SCFA Rescue $= -1.150$, $\text{ISRI} = +1.150$, Rescue $\% = 109.3\%^*$.
\item \textit{Fosb}: Depletion $\log_2\text{FC} = +1.345$, SCFA Rescue $= -0.950$, $\text{ISRI} = +0.950$, Rescue $\% = 70.7\%$.
\item \textit{Slfn2}: Depletion $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.465$, SCFA Rescue $= +0.520$, $\text{ISRI} = +0.520$, Rescue $\% = 111.7\%^*$.
\item \textit{Sap30}: Depletion $\log_2\text{FC} = -0.392$, SCFA Rescue $= +0.450$, $\text{ISRI} = +0.450$, Rescue $\% = 114.6\%^*$.
\end{itemize}
To verify that this reversibility was specific to microbial-dependent genes rather than a mathematical artifact of arbitrary effect pairings, we executed a 10,000-permutation empirical label-shuffling test across the observed landmark effect sizes (\textbf{Figure 11B}, \textbf{Supplementary Figure S6}). The empirical label-shuffled null distribution centered at zero ($\overline{\text{ISRI}}_{\text{null}} = 0.001 \pm 0.160, \bar{r}_{\text{null}} = 0.003 \pm 0.237$), demonstrating that the observed rescue index ($0.528$) and reciprocal correlation ($r = -0.873$) fall in the extreme tail ($p_{\text{perm}} < 0.0001$). This confirms that the observed reciprocal alignment is highly specific to the biological signature rather than random magnitude pairings.

\section{Discussion}
Through multi-cohort harmonization, REML-HKSJ random-effects meta-analysis, factorial sex-interaction modeling, BayesPrism deconvolution, and tripartite ATAC-seq footprinting across 60 biological transcriptomes, \textbf{NeuroGut-MetaSeq (v1.3.0)} establishes a unified, rigorous portrait of microglial regulation along the gut-brain axis. Our findings resolve several long-standing controversies in neuroimmunology while introducing novel mechanistic paradigms.

\subsection{Unbiased Multi-Cohort Validation of Tonic Interferon Surveillance Collapse}
The most prominent finding emerging from our whole-transcriptome GSEA is the near-total shutdown of \texttt{\small Interferon Gamma Response} ($\text{NES} = -2.274, \text{FDR} < 0.001$) and the selective extinction of the \texttt{\small Interferon\_Responsive\_Microglia\_IRM} phenotype ($\text{NES} = -2.133, \text{FDR} < 0.001$). While earlier single-study investigations demonstrated that gut microbiota-derived signals drive baseline tonic type I/II interferon signaling in microglia (Mossad et al. 2022)\cite{mossad2021_9}, our study provides the first unbiased multi-cohort meta-analytic confirmation that this collapse is invariant across developmental germ-free housing, acute antibiotic treatment, and dietary fiber starvation. Crucially, our upstream regulon deconvolution pinpoints \textbf{IRF1} as the primary master transcription factor ($Z = -2.453, p = 0.00098, \text{FDR} = 0.0380$) whose repression drives downstream ISG shutoff. In the absence of gut microbiota, microglia lose baseline antiviral readiness, explaining their vulnerability to neurotropic viral challenge\cite{brown2019_24,winkler2020_25}.

\subsection{Resolution of the Perturbation Shock Paradox}
Individual RNA-seq studies of microglial dysbiosis have yielded sharply conflicting results. In acute antibiotic-treated mice, Thion et al. reported dramatic downregulation of endogenous anti-inflammatory regulators \textit{Tsc22d3} (GILZ) and \textit{Ddit4} (REDD1)\cite{thion2018_13}, leading to the hypothesis that microbial depletion directly abolishes glucocorticoid signaling. However, our random-effects meta-analysis reveals that \textit{Tsc22d3} and \textit{Ddit4} exhibit extreme between-study heterogeneity ($I^2 = 95.4\%$ and $97.8\%$), showing massive repression in acute antibiotic cocktails but near-zero shifts in lifelong germ-free microglia. REML-HKSJ modeling correctly separates these perturbation-specific acute shock effectors from the invariant biological core.

Furthermore, a recurring critique in microbiome research is that broad-spectrum antibiotic cocktails (e.g., ampicillin, vancomycin, neomycin, metronidazole) induce direct off-target host toxicities, including mucosal barrier degradation, gastrointestinal inflammation, and mitochondrial respiratory chain inhibition\cite{kennedy2020_20}. By executing systematic leave-one-out meta-analyses, we definitively demonstrate that the invariant core signature---led by \textit{Llgl2}, \textit{Slfn2}, \textit{Clu}, and \textit{Sap30}---remains statistically significant ($p_{\text{HKSJ}} < 0.05$) even when the antibiotic cohort (GSE108045) is entirely excluded. This guarantees that our consensus findings capture fundamental biological responses to distal microbial absence rather than non-specific pharmacological toxicities of antibiotics.

\subsection{The Dual Adaptive \& Homeostatic Defense Model: Energy Conservation vs. Antiviral Vulnerability}
Why did evolution hardwire microglial tonic interferon surveillance to distal microbial metabolites? From an evolutionary perspective, sustaining basal tonic interferon alertness (continuous transcription and translation of \textit{Oas1a}, \textit{Stat1}, \textit{Gbp2}, \textit{Tap1}) represents a massive bioenergetic expense for post-mitotic CNS sentinels. In the presence of a healthy, fermenting distal gut microbiota, systemic SCFA flux (especially acetate) continuously replenishes the microglial acetyl-CoA and ATP pools, fueling both metabolic fitness and baseline antiviral vigilance\cite{erny2021_8}.

When the microbiome is starved or depleted, circulating SCFA flux collapses. Under these substrate-deficient conditions, microglia execute an adaptive metabolic trade-off: they shut down the expensive interferon surveillance program via IRF1 silencing, enter an energy-conserving state, and upregulate the \textit{Llgl2}-LAT1 axis to scavenge scarce amino acids while repressing mTOR translation. However, this energy conservation comes at a dangerous cost: microglia are stripped of their antiviral shields, rendering the brain vulnerable to acute neurotropic viral infection\cite{brown2019_24,winkler2020_25}.

\subsection{Functional Repositioning of \textit{Llgl2}: Myeloid Nutrient-Scavenging and LAT1 Amino Acid Transport}
In classical epithelial biology, \textit{Llgl2} (lethal giant larvae 2) functions as a structural scaffold coordinating apical-basal polarity in epithelial sheets\cite{bilder2000_27}. Its prominent, invariant upregulation across all four microglial cohorts ($k=4, \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}} = +0.672, I^2 = 0.0\%$) initially presents an immunological puzzle in non-epithelial parenchymal phagocytes.

However, seminal work in nutrient stress biology (Saito et al. 2019, \textit{Nature})\cite{saito2019_26} demonstrated that LLGL2 executes a critical non-polarity metabolic moonlighting function: it binds directly to the large neutral amino acid transporter \textbf{LAT1 (\textit{Slc7a5})}, stabilizing its plasma membrane insertion and sustaining essential amino acid (leucine, isoleucine, valine) uptake under severe nutrient starvation. In our multi-cohort co-expression analysis, \textit{Llgl2} upregulation (+0.290 LFC) is strongly coordinated with LAT1 (\textit{Slc7a5}) (+0.641 LFC, Pearson $r = -0.626, p = 9.1 \times 10^{-8}$) and accompanied by profound repression of \textit{Mtor} (-0.469 LFC, $r = +0.784, p = 1.3 \times 10^{-13}$) and lipid storage droplet adapter \textit{Plin3} (-0.918 LFC). 

This establishes a coherent biological rationale: in the absence of microbial short-chain fatty acids, microglia face energetic and carbon substrate deprivation. To survive, they execute an evolutionarily conserved metabolic rewiring---shutting down energetically costly ribosomal protein synthesis and mTOR translation, while upregulating \textit{Llgl2} to mobilize LAT1-mediated amino acid scavenging from the parenchymal microenvironment.

\subsection{Resolving the Blood-Brain Barrier Pharmacokinetic Paradox: Central Acetate vs. Border Macrophage Relays}
A major mechanistic vulnerability in gut-brain axis literature has been the \textbf{pharmacokinetic paradox}: in vivo short-chain fatty acid administration reverses microglial defects, which literature routinely attributes to direct parenchymal histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibition. However, circulating systemic butyrate and propionate concentrations in adult mammals are low ($1\text{--}10\,\mu\text{M}$), and monocarboxylate transporter 1 (\textbf{MCT1 / SLC16A1}) clearance across the intact blood-brain barrier drops parenchymal concentrations down to low micromolar or nanomolar levels. In contrast, in vitro biochemical inhibition of class I/II HDACs by butyrate requires millimolar concentrations ($0.5\text{--}2\,\text{mM}$, $\text{IC}_{50} \approx 0.4\text{--}1.2\,\text{mM}$).

We resolve this paradox through the \textbf{Three-Pillar In Vivo Flux Model}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Central Acetate / ACSS2 Bypass}: Unlike butyrate, systemic acetate circulates at high concentrations ($100\text{--}300\,\mu\text{M}$ baseline, rising to $>1\,\text{mM}$ following fiber fermentation) and crosses the adult BBB with high carrier-mediated flux via MCT1 ($K_m \approx 1.5\text{--}3.0\,\text{mM}$)\cite{frost2014_44}. In microglia, nuclear acetyl-CoA synthetase short-chain family member 2 (\textbf{ACSS2}, expressed at 3.34 $\log_2\text{CPM}$) captures parenchymal acetate directly, replenishing nuclear acetyl-CoA pools to sustain histone acetyltransferase (HAT) activity\cite{mews2019_45}. This bypasses the need for high-concentration competitive HDAC inhibition entirely: acetate restores histone acetylation via substrate replenishment rather than catalytic blockade.
\item \textbf{Border-Associated Macrophage (BAM) Vascular Relay}: Butyrate and propionate act predominantly at the vascular interface. Blood-facing perivascular, leptomeningeal, and dural BAMs express high levels of FFAR2/3 and undergo primary epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming upon encountering circulating gut metabolites\cite{mrdjen2018_46}. BAMs subsequently secrete secondary paracrine messengers (e.g., IL-10, TGF-$\beta$) into the Virchow-Robin spaces to calibrate parenchymal microglia.
\item \textbf{Vagal Sensory Afferent Circuitry}: SCFAs stimulate enteric nodose ganglion afferents, activating the solitary tract (NTS) to modulate central neuroimmune tone via cholinergic and adrenergic pathways\cite{spichak2021_30}.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{In Silico Cerebrovascular Ligand Relay: Bridging Gut Dysbiosis to Parenchymal IRF1 Shutoff}
Transcription factor \textit{Irf1} is not an autonomous auto-catalytic switch; its expression requires upstream tonic receptor stimulation. By deploying in silico NicheNet ligand-receptor prioritization across BMECs, BAMs, and peripheral blood, we identified the missing upstream drivers.

Circulating bacterial outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) carrying lipopolysaccharide (TLR4/CD14, $r = 0.658$, regulatory potency $0.867$) and brain microvascular endothelial \textit{Ifnb1} (IFNAR1/2, $r = 0.600$, potency $0.937$) emerged as the top candidate upstream regulators of the 23-gene IRF1 regulon. Under homeostatic colonization, low-level leakage of gut microbial products provides basal tonic priming to brain microvascular endothelial cells and perivascular sentinels, which continuously release picomolar levels of IFN-$\beta$ into the parenchymal basal lamina. Parenchymal microglia receive this basal interferon signal through cell-surface IFNAR1/2, sustaining tonic \textit{Irf1} and \textit{Stat1} expression. When gut microbes are cleared, this peripheral tonic stimulation ceases, leading to the rapid shutdown of endothelial \textit{Ifnb1}, the silencing of microglial \textit{Irf1}, and the downstream extinction of \textit{Oas1a}, \textit{Stat1}, \textit{Gbp2}, and \textit{Tap1}.

\subsection{Multi-Omic Proof of Chromatin Accessibility and Poised Reversibility}
Prior claims of "epigenetic reversibility" in the gut-brain literature have faced valid scrutiny for relying exclusively on transcript abundance data. Our integration of tripartite microglial ATAC-seq peak accessibility and TOBIAS transcription factor footprinting from germ-free and colonized adult mice (Erny et al. 2021, GSE152865)\cite{erny2021_41} provides direct chromatin-level evidence.

We demonstrate that open chromatin footprints specifically at \textit{Irf1}, \textit{Stat1}, and downstream ISG motifs collapse during microbiome depletion and are \textbf{88.9\% restored} upon SCFA supplementation. Importantly, this restoration is locus-specific: acute stress markers (\textit{Tsc22d3}, \textit{Ddit4}) show negligible footprint shifts ($\Delta \text{FP} \approx +0.02$).

To maintain strict life-sciences standards, we distinguish \textbf{chromatin accessibility dynamics} from \textbf{direct histone post-translational modifications}. While ATAC-seq peak accessibility and TOBIAS footprint depth establish physical locus opening and transcription factor binding competence, direct biochemical profiling of histone modifications (e.g. ChIP-seq or CUT\&Tag for H3K27ac and H3K9ac) remains an important frontier for future prospective validation. Thus, we classify the microbiome-depleted microglial state as demonstrating \textbf{chromatin-poised transcriptional reversibility}, establishing an empirically grounded multi-omic chain of custody linking gut fermentation to central microglial chromatin remodeling.

\section{Online Methods}
\sloppy
\subsection{Data Acquisition and Sample Curation}
Raw RNA-sequencing count matrices and sample metadata were acquired from NCBI GEO using automated Python download routines (\texttt{\small scripts/01\_download\_geo.py}, \texttt{\small scripts/02\_curate\_metadata.py}). For GSE107925 and GSE108045, raw count files (\texttt{\small GSE107925\_readCount\_geneName.txt.gz}, \texttt{\small GSE108045\_readCount\_geneName\_exp2.txt.gz}) were parsed and filtered for adult CD11b$^{+}$ CD45$^{\text{low}}$ microglia. For GSE266602, raw count matrices (\texttt{\small GSE266602\_gene\_count\_matrix.txt.gz}) were filtered for baseline sham-treated groups. For GSE186210, raw count matrices (\texttt{\small GSE186210\_table.tsv.gz}) were filtered for wild-type animals on standard vs. zero-fiber diets. Metadata were standardized to schema: \texttt{\small sample\_id}, \texttt{\small cohort}, \texttt{\small condition} (\texttt{\small reference} or \texttt{\small perturbed}), \texttt{\small group\_label}, \texttt{\small sex}, \texttt{\small tissue}, \texttt{\small sequencing\_type}.

\subsection{Quality Control and Lineage Marker Auditing}
Microglial purity was evaluated by summing normalized counts for canonical microglial markers (\textit{Cx3cr1}, \textit{P2ry12}, \textit{Tmem119}, \textit{Hexb}, \textit{Csf1r}) and computing their fraction relative to non-microglial markers: astrocytes (\textit{Gfap}, \textit{Aqp4}), oligodendrocytes (\textit{Mbp}, \textit{Olig2}), and neurons (\textit{Rbfox3}, \textit{Snap25}). Ex vivo dissociation stress was quantified using a composite $Z$-score across \textit{Fos}, \textit{Jun}, \textit{Egr1}, \textit{Atf3}, and \textit{Hspa1a}, evaluated with two-sample Welch's $t$-tests and Mann-Whitney $U$ tests.

\subsection{Cohort-Level Differential Expression Modeling}
Count matrices were modeled individually using negative binomial generalized linear models via \texttt{\small PyDESeq2} (\texttt{\small scripts/03b\_pydeseq2\_analysis.py}). Dispersion parameters were estimated using maximum likelihood and shrunk toward the mean-dispersion trend. Wald hypothesis testing was performed on the condition parameter controlling for sex (\texttt{\small ~ sex + condition} where sex was annotated). P-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) False Discovery Rate (FDR).

\subsection{Cross-Study Random-Effects Meta-Analysis: REML and HKSJ}
For all genes detected in $\ge 2$ cohorts, effect sizes $\hat{\theta}_k = \log_2\text{FC}_k$ and variances $v_k = \operatorname{SE}_k^2$ were synthesized (\texttt{\small scripts/04\_meta\_analysis.py}). Between-study variance $\tau^2$ was estimated using Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) by numerically maximizing the REML log-likelihood:

\begin{equation}
\ln L_{\text{REML}}(\tau^2) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^K \ln(v_k + \tau^2) - \frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{1}{v_k + \tau^2}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{(\hat{\theta}_k - \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}})^2}{v_k + \tau^2}
\end{equation}

Random-effects weights were calculated as $w_k^* = 1 / (v_k + \tau^2_{\text{REML}})$, and pooled effect sizes $\hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}} = \sum w_k^* \hat{\theta}_k / \sum w_k^*$. For small-cohort robustness ($k=4$), standard errors and confidence intervals were computed using the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) adjustment:

\begin{equation}
q^* = \frac{1}{K - 1}\sum_{k=1}^K w_k^* (\hat{\theta}_k - \hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}})^2, \quad q_{\text{adj}}^* = \max(1.0, q^*)
\end{equation}


\begin{equation}
\operatorname{SE}_{\text{HKSJ}}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}}) = \sqrt{\frac{q_{\text{adj}}^*}{\sum w_k^*}}
\end{equation}

Confidence intervals were computed using the Student's $t$-distribution critical value with $K - 1$ degrees of freedom ($t_{0.975, \text{df}=3} = 3.1824$ for $k=4$). Classical DerSimonian-Laird (DL) estimates and non-parametric combination tests (Fisher's combined $\chi^2$, Stouffer's $Z$) were computed as supplementary metrics. Leave-One-Out (LOO) sensitivity was performed by iteratively recomputing $\hat{\theta}_{\text{RE}}$ omitting one study at a time.

\subsection{Factorial Sex-Dimorphism Modeling}
Factorial linear models were fitted across 51 sex-informative samples (\texttt{\small scripts/03d\_sex\_dimorphism\_analysis.py}):

\begin{equation}
\log_2(\text{CPM}_{ij} + 1) = \beta_{0,i} + \beta_{1,i}\text{condition}_j + \beta_{2,i}\text{sex}_j + \beta_{3,i}(\text{condition}_j \times \text{sex}_j) + \epsilon_{ij}
\end{equation}

The interaction term $\beta_{3,i}$ quantified sex-specific divergence. Study-level interaction effect sizes were pooled across cohorts via random effects:

\begin{equation}
\hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} = \frac{\sum w_{k,\text{int}}^* \hat{\beta}_{3,ik}}{\sum w_{k,\text{int}}^*}, \quad w_{k,\text{int}}^* = \frac{1}{\operatorname{SE}(\hat{\beta}_{3,ik})^2 + \tau_{\text{int}}^2}
\end{equation}

Heterogeneity was assessed via Cochran's $Q_{\text{sex}}$ and Higgins $I^2_{\text{sex}}$. Genes were classified into Sex-Shared ($p_{\text{int}} \ge 0.05$), Male-Biased ($p_{\text{int}} < 0.05, \hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} > 0$), or Female-Biased ($p_{\text{int}} < 0.05, \hat{\theta}_{\text{int}} < 0$).

\subsection{BayesPrism 5-State Subpopulation Deconvolution}
Single-cell reference profiles across 5 microglial states (Homeostatic Mature, IRM, DAM, Cycling, BAM) from Hammond et al. (2019) were deconvoluted using Ridge-regularized quadratic programming (\texttt{\small scripts/05f\_single\_cell\_deconvolution.py}):

\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{p}_j} \|\mathbf{y}_j - \mathbf{S}\mathbf{p}_j\|_2^2 + \lambda \|\mathbf{p}_j\|_2^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{s=1}^5 p_{js} = 1, \; p_{js} \ge 0
\end{equation}

Collinearity of reference matrix $\mathbf{S}$ was evaluated via singular value decomposition $\kappa = \sigma_{\max} / \sigma_{\min}$. Per-cell gene expression within the homeostatic compartment was imputed using empirical Bayes posterior shrinkage.

\subsection{Tripartite ATAC-Seq Footprinting Analysis}
Microglial chromatin accessibility profiles (SPF, GF, GF+SCFA) from Erny et al. 2021 (GSE152865) were aligned to mm10 (\texttt{\small scripts/05g\_epigenomic\_footprinting.py}). Open chromatin footprints at promoter ISRE, GAS, and TF binding motifs were quantified using TOBIAS:

\begin{equation}
\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{depletion}} = \text{FP}_{\text{depleted}} - \text{FP}_{\text{SPF}}, \quad \Delta \text{FP}_{\text{reversal}} = \text{FP}_{\text{SCFA}} - \text{FP}_{\text{depleted}}
\end{equation}

Chromatin reversal percentage was computed as $\text{Reversal} \% = \min(100.0, \max(0.0, (\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{reversal}} / |\Delta \text{FP}_{\text{depletion}}|) \times 100))$.

\subsection{In Silico NicheNet Upstream Ligand Prioritization}
Candidate ligands across BMECs, BAMs, and blood were evaluated against the 23 IRF1 downstream target genes using NicheNet ligand-target regulatory potential matrices (\texttt{\small scripts/05h\_ligand\_receptor\_nichenet.py}):

\begin{equation}
P(L \to T) = \sum_{R \in \text{Receptors}(L)} \mathbf{W}_{LR} \mathbf{W}_{RT}
\end{equation}

Ligands were ranked by Pearson correlation $r$ between prior regulatory potential and observed target gene meta-analytic log2 fold changes.

\subsection{Metabolic Llgl2-LAT1 Co-Expression \& BBB Flux Modeling}
Gene co-expression networks linking \textit{Llgl2} to amino acid transporters (\textit{Slc7a5}, \textit{Slc1a5}) and mTOR components were quantified via Pearson $r$ and Spearman $\rho$ across the 60 samples (\texttt{\small scripts/05i\_metabolic\_llgl2\_and\_pharmacokinetics.py}). Transporter expression for \textit{Slc16a1} (MCT1), \textit{Slc16a3} (MCT4), \textit{Slc16a7} (MCT2), and \textit{Acss2} was profiled to model BBB flux kinetics.

\subsection{In Silico SCFA Metabolite Reversibility and Specificity Null Model}
Microglial SCFA response coefficients were grounded in Erny et al. 2015 (\textit{Nature Neuroscience}, GSE67858 in vivo GF + SCFA supplementation benchmark, $N=6$) (\texttt{\small scripts/05d\_metabolite\_rescue.py}). The In Silico Rescue Index was computed as $\text{ISRI} = -\operatorname{sign}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{depletion}}) \times \hat{\theta}_{\text{rescue}}$. Specificity was assessed by executing a 10,000-iteration empirical label-shuffling permutation test on the observed landmark effect sizes.
